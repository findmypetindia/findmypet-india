Safety guidance

Immediate safety

What should you do first?

Stay safe while you try to get help for the animal.

1

Keep yourself safe

Do not enter traffic or approach an aggressive, frightened or wild animal. Avoid putting yourself in danger.

2

Share the location

Note the city, area, nearby landmark and road direction. Share a photo only from a safe distance.

3

Contact suitable help

Use the organisation directory to contact a relevant rescue group or local animal welfare service. Confirm availability directly.

4

Do not force treatment

Do not give human medicines, food or water to an unconscious or severely injured animal unless a veterinarian instructs you.

Free Rescue First

Help should be clear and transparent.

We aim to prioritise verified free rescue options where available. Government services, free NGOs and paid private providers must be clearly identified. Paid treatment should never be presented as free.

What happens after submission?

Your report receives a complaint ID. If an approved on-call partner matches the animal type and reported city or coverage area, the request appears in that partner’s secure dashboard. The partner must accept it before it is treated as accepted. A dashboard assignment does not mean a team has travelled or arrived. Please also contact local help directly in urgent situations.
